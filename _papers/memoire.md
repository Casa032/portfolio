title: Exchange rate volatility
---

Injections as a monetary policy instrument of the central bank to meet the growing demand for the dollar on the foreign exchange market in recent years have repeatedly been the subject of debate in the economic sphere. More than one argues that this is an ineffective policy with barely visible results. Indeed, in the analysis of the bilateral nominal exchange rate between the United States and Haiti, the gourde is not doing better following the interventions tolerated in order to fight the disease of depreciation.

Therefore, in order to provide an element of response and move this discussion forward, we will focus in the following lines on the effectiveness of central bank interventions in the foreign exchange market and exchange rate volatility in Haiti for the period from October 2008 to March 2020. The econometric models used are based on the Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity (ARCH) model, which allows better modeling of time series whose variance varies over time.

From the results found, it can be stated that net interventions have little effect on the movement of the exchange rate and are ineffective in reducing volatility in the market. Similarly, when analyzing the behavior of other variables, we see that poor economic performance is not beneficial to the external stability of the gourde.
